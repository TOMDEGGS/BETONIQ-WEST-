**For Tom to use when explaining this to local business people, agents, or partners — no tech jargon.**

Think of all 7 of these as different ways of saying the same thing: *"We're not just telling you our payment system is safe — we're letting anyone check it, for free, in public."* That's the whole idea. Investors and engineers don't trust claims, they trust proof they can check themselves.

## 1. The public "break my math" challenge
We put up a public dare: "if you think you can trick our system into letting someone spend the same money twice, try it — here's exactly how the system works, go ahead and attack it." It's like a locksmith putting his lock in a shop window and inviting anyone to pick it. If nobody can break it after real experts try, that's worth more than us just saying "trust us."

## 2. The machine-checked proof
This is the big one. We didn't just write down on paper "this can't be cheated." We built a computer model of exactly how our payment system behaves, and had a specialized verification program (the same kind of tool used to check nuclear plant control software and spacecraft code) try *every single possible order* that a cheat attempt could happen in — over a thousand different scenarios. The result: zero ways to cheat found. Then, to prove the test itself wasn't rigged or too easy, we deliberately broke our own system on purpose and ran the same test — and this time it caught the cheat immediately, in 5 steps. That second part is important: it proves the test actually works, it's not just giving us a "pass" no matter what.

## 3. The 5-part video series
Instead of one long video trying to prove everything at once, we're making 5 short video clips, each one knocking down exactly one specific doubt a skeptic might have — "what if you fake the signal," "what if you copy the payment," "what if two people try to cheat at the same second," "can you catch who cheated," and "show me you actually tested this, not just talked about it." Short, sharp, one point each — easier for people to watch and share.

## 4. The public timestamp
We took a fingerprint (a unique code) of our proof document and registered it permanently and for free on the Bitcoin network — the same trick used to prove nobody can secretly go back and rewrite the document later and pretend they said it earlier. It's like getting a notary stamp, except it's done by thousands of computers worldwide instead of one notary, and nobody can fake or backdate it.

## 5. The public scoreboard
A simple public page that will track every single attempt anyone makes to break our system — who tried, what they tried, whether they succeeded or failed. Right now it's empty because nobody has tried yet, but the moment someone does, it's all out in the open. This builds trust the same way a restaurant's public health inspection score does — nothing hidden.

## 6. Personalized short clips for specific investors
Instead of sending the exact same video to every investor we're courting, we're cutting four slightly different short versions — each one opens by speaking directly to what that particular investor's fund cares about most (one cares about hardware, one cares about the engineering risk, one cares about it as national infrastructure, one cares about the cryptography itself). Same proof, four different doors into the same room.
